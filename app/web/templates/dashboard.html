How to read this

Outgo is living spend plus debt payments. Income and outgo leave out transfers between envelopes and the other exclusions listed on the Method tab. Dotted markers are life events; hover for the label. The gap between the income line and the dotted spend-plus-debt line is the month’s net. Months where the dotted line sits above income were funded from savings or a one-off inflow. Spikes in income are bonuses, tax refunds, stimulus or severance; spikes in spend are usually a tax bill, a trip or a project. Hover a dot along the top for the event behind a step change.

    Category shape, year over year

    • up to three stars, each on its own scale
    • a spoke is a category, a ring a year
    • current year solid, older years fade
    • click a year in the legend to hide it
    How to read this

    Up to three stars so the small categories are readable: each has its own dollar scale. A spoke is a category, a ring is a year. The current year is drawn solid; earlier years fade with age. Click a year in the legend to hide or show it on every star. Read a star as a fingerprint: the current year’s solid outline against the faded outlines of earlier years. A spoke that grows year over year is a category whose share of life grew; a spoke that shrinks is one that was tamed. The stars are made from your own categories: those with spending are ranked by their total over all years and dealt into the largest third, the middle third and the smallest third (fewer stars when there are fewer than nine categories, and never one with fewer than three spokes). Scales differ between stars, so compare shapes within a star, not across them. Switch to real dollars to see whether a growing spoke is more than inflation.

        How the numbers were made, what was removed, and the choices to revisit if a figure looks wrong.

        How the numbers are made

        Budget month, not calendar monthRows count in the budget month EveryDollar filed them in.

        EveryDollar files transactions into budget months, and about one row in thirteen sits in a different budget month than its calendar date. Everything here uses the budget month, so a late-January rent payment filed into February stays in February, matching what the budget showed at the time.

        Nominal versus real amountsReal restates every month in one month's prices.

        Nominal is what the bank saw. Real restates each month's amounts in {{ span.cpi_base_long }} prices by multiplying by the ratio of the {{ span.cpi_base_long }} price index to that month's price index. For US households the documented source is CPI-U (all urban consumers, all items, seasonally adjusted, series CPIAUCSL from FRED); other countries use their own consumer price index, entered on the Settings page. Use real to compare lifestyle across years; use nominal to reconcile against statements.

        What was taken outTransfers and other non-spending rows removed before any total.

        Transfers between envelopes appear in EveryDollar as a negative row in one item and a positive row in another, so summing them would inflate both income and spend. They are recognised by merchant text, by item name and by amount pairing, and removed. What follows is generated from the data loaded, so it lists only what this household's rules actually removed.

        Item namesRenamed envelopes are merged through the mapping.

        EveryDollar items get renamed and recreated, so the same envelope can appear under several names. Each original name is mapped to a canonical item and a category; the mapping file lives beside the ledger and is the thing to edit (Settings) if a category looks wrong.

        RefreshingEvery figure is rebuilt from the stored exports.

        Each month the two EveryDollar exports for the closed budget month are added to the set, the ledger and these files are rebuilt, and the page redraws from them. Nothing on the page is typed in by hand, so a corrected export changes every dependent figure at once.

        Reading the colorsGreen and red mark direction, not judgment.

        Green and red mark direction, not judgment: green is money kept or a category under its envelope, red is money drawn down or an envelope exceeded. Chart series take the brand palette in order (series 1 through 8 in the theme).

        NotesEveryDollar notes are kept in the ledger, never shown here.

        The ledger carries every EveryDollar note, but notes are not shown on this page, because they can hold private details such as medication and people's names.
