Income against outgo, by budget month

Outgo is living spend plus debt payments. Income and outgo leave out transfers between envelopes and the other exclusions listed on the Method tab. Dotted markers are life events; hover for the label.

How to read this

The gap between the income line and the dotted spend-plus-debt line is the month’s net. Months where the dotted line sits above income were funded from savings or a one-off inflow. Spikes in income are bonuses, tax refunds, stimulus or severance; spikes in spend are usually a tax bill, a trip or a project. Hover a dot along the top for the event behind a step change.

Month-close review · written by Claude from the figures on this page

Reading the month

Prose analysis of the closed month against the month before it and the twelve-month trend, with plan changes to consider. Regenerated each month when the new exports are loaded; every number it cites is in the tables below.

How this is made

After each month's exports are added, Claude reads the planned-versus-spent table, the category gaps against plan and against the trailing average, and the ledger's clean monthly totals, then writes this section. It is analysis, not a record: the numbers it quotes come from the datasets, and the judgments are a starting point for your own.

What to look at before setting next month

The three categories most over plan and the three most under, with how far each sat from its own twelve-month average.

    Over and under plan, by category

    Spent minus planned for the month. Bars to the right went over the envelope; bars to the left came in under. Income is shown as received minus expected.

    How to read this

    Red bars went over their envelope; green came in under. A large red bar on a planned-zero category means an unplanned expense, not an overrun. Income is reversed: red means less arrived than expected.

    Against the trailing twelve months

    Spent this month minus the average of the twelve months before it, by category. This is the signal for whether a plan number should move.

    How to read this

    This compares the month with its own recent history rather than with the plan. A category that is over plan but at its twelve-month average has a plan problem; one that is at plan but far above its average had an unusual month.

    Category detail

    Planned and spent for the month, the trailing twelve-month average, and a suggested plan for next month: the twelve-month average rounded up to the nearest ten, except for Housing, Debt and Insurance, which keep the current plan.

    How to read this

    Suggested plan is the twelve-month average rounded up to the nearest ten dollars, so a plan set from it would have covered a typical month. Housing, Debt and Insurance are contractual and keep their current plan. Treat it as a starting number, not a rule.

    The three ratios, month by month

    Giving as a share of gross (floor and target lines), joy-envelope funding as a share of gross (target line), and housing plus utilities as a share of take-home (cap line). Twelve-month trailing averages, so a three-paycheck month or a lump payment does not whip the line around.

    Giving / grossJoy funding / grossHousing + utilities / take-home
    How to read this

    A solid line above its dashed target is good for giving and joy, and bad for housing. The lines are twelve-month trailing averages, so a change in behaviour takes a few months to show. Gross is the stated annual salary spread evenly across months; take-home is the net paychecks that actually landed, so the housing ratio is honest about months with two versus three paychecks only in the trailing view.

    Year

    How the numbers were made, what was removed, and the choices to revisit if a figure looks wrong.

    How to read this

    Budget month, not calendar month

    EveryDollar files transactions into budget months, and about one row in thirteen sits in a different budget month than its calendar date. Everything here uses the budget month, so a late-January rent payment filed into February stays in February, matching what the budget showed at the time.

    Nominal versus real amounts

    Nominal is what the bank saw. Real restates each month's amounts in {{ span.cpi_base_long }} prices by multiplying by the ratio of the {{ span.cpi_base_long }} price index to that month's price index. For US households the documented source is CPI-U (all urban consumers, all items, seasonally adjusted, series CPIAUCSL from FRED); other countries use their own consumer price index, entered on the Settings page. Use real to compare lifestyle across years; use nominal to reconcile against statements.

    What was taken out

    Transfers between envelopes appear in EveryDollar as a negative row in one item and a positive row in another, so summing them would inflate both income and spend. They are recognised by merchant text, by item name and by amount pairing, and removed. What follows is generated from the data loaded, so it lists only what this household's rules actually removed.

    Item names

    EveryDollar items get renamed and recreated, so the same envelope can appear under several names. Each original name is mapped to a canonical item and a category; the mapping file lives beside the ledger and is the thing to edit (Settings) if a category looks wrong.

    Refreshing

    Each month the two EveryDollar exports for the closed budget month are added to the set, the ledger and these files are rebuilt, and the page redraws from them. Nothing on the page is typed in by hand, so a corrected export changes every dependent figure at once.

    Reading the colors

    Green and red mark direction, not judgment: green is money kept or a category under its envelope, red is money drawn down or an envelope exceeded. Chart series take the brand palette in order (series 1 through 8 in the theme).

    Notes

    The ledger carries every EveryDollar note, but notes are not shown on this page, because they can hold private details such as medication and people's names.

    Reconciliation

    Every envelope's transactions should sum to its Spent in the BudgetItems export; any that do not are listed here.
