      Planned and spent are your budget tool's own figures, so moves between envelopes count as they did in the app; the other tabs use the cleaned ledger.

      {{ glyph('net-worth') }} Net worth

      • what you own less what you owe, at the month-end
      • the change, split into what you did and what happened to you

      How to read this

      Net worth is every account's month-end balance (Settings → Accounts; entered at Close a month), assets less debts, leaving out accounts marked not in net worth. Its change since last month is split in two. What you did is the month's income less living spend: what was kept plus what was paid on debts, since paying a loan down is the household's own doing (and, once stated, pre-tax saving such as a 401(k)). What happened to you is the rest: investments and the home's estimate moving, interest charged, and money moving into or out of accounts this page does not track. An account's first month is shown on its own, so adding one is not read as progress. A debt with no balance entered is estimated, and the figure says so. One thing lands on the wrong side: money your budget counts as spent that goes into an account tracked here (a savings envelope paid into a savings account) shows under what happened, not what you did. Excluding home takes a home's typed estimate out, since it moves the total more than any month's decisions.

      Decisions in force

      • the decisions in force in the latest month, and how each went
      • the share kept, over the last six months of checks
      How to read this

      Decisions are written down at the check-in's Next month step and asked about at the next one. Where a decision names a category, a budget item or a debt and an amount, the app checks it against the month's budget: a spend cap against what was spent, a save against what was put in, an extra payment against the usual payment, a plan change against next month's planned figure. The household's own verdict is the one that counts; "partly" is half. A month whose files are not in yet is waiting, never "not kept".

      When the debts are gone

      • each debt's month-end balance and its change
      • the month it is paid off at last month's payment (est.)
      How to read this

      For each debt with a month-end balance and a linked budget item (Settings → Accounts), the months left at last month's payment to that item, less escrow, at its APR: the payoff month is when the balance reaches zero if every month's payment is the same. With no APR the date ignores interest, so it is the earliest the debt could be gone ("at least"). A card's date assumes no new charges. When last month had no payment the 3-month average is used, and says so. Each date moves as payments change: "3 months sooner: you paid extra". It is an estimate for the check-in, not a loan schedule.

      How it is worked out

      The pace is the monthly net (income less living spend and debt payments) averaged over the last twelve budget months. Kept adds the payments each debt frees once it is paid off. Net worth is today's, plus what is kept, plus what the debt payments take off the balances.       {{ glyph('emergency-fund') }} Emergency fund

      • if income stopped: months of outgo the emergency savings would cover
      How to read this

      Emergency money is the month-end balance of the asset accounts marked Emergency (Settings → Accounts; cash and savings accounts are, unless you switch one off, such as a holiday fund). Outgo is living spend plus debt payments, averaged over the last twelve budget months, so one heavy month does not swing it. Total outgo is used, not a bare-bones budget: it is the honest figure.       Debt against income

      • debt payments ÷ gross pay (the stated salary ÷ 12)
      • the same payments ÷ take-home, after it
      How to read this

      Debt against income (lenders call it debt-to-income) is the share of gross pay already spoken for by debt payments, the figure lenders quote; take-home is what the household actually feels, so it is shown after it. All debt counts every row with Flow debt. Housing counts the housing categories (Pipeline rules, goal_housing_categories: Housing and Utilities unless changed) plus any debt of type mortgage, each payment once; rent counts here and not under all debt. Lenders' housing figure usually leaves utilities out, so take Utilities out of that rule to compare with one. A cap shows only when you set one on Settings → Goals (dti_cap, housing_dti_cap); the app has no opinion of its own.

          Year

          How the numbers were made, what was removed, and the choices to revisit if a figure looks wrong.

          How the numbers are made

          Budget month, not calendar monthRows count in the budget month your budget tool filed them in.

          Your budget tool files transactions into budget months, and about one row in thirteen sits in a different budget month than its calendar date. Everything here uses the budget month, so a late-January rent payment filed into February stays in February, matching what the budget showed at the time.

          Nominal versus real amountsReal restates every month in one month's prices.

          Nominal is what the bank saw. Real restates each month's amounts in {{ span.cpi_base_long }} prices by multiplying by the ratio of the {{ span.cpi_base_long }} price index to that month's price index. For US households the documented source is CPI-U (all urban consumers, all items, seasonally adjusted, series CPIAUCSL from FRED); other countries use their own consumer price index, entered on the Settings page. Use real to compare lifestyle across years; use nominal to reconcile against statements.

          What was taken outTransfers and other non-spending rows removed before any total.

          Transfers between envelopes appear in the export as a negative row in one item and a positive row in another, so summing them would inflate both income and spend. They are recognised by merchant text, by item name and by amount pairing, and removed. What follows is generated from the data loaded, so it lists only what this household's rules actually removed.

          Item namesRenamed envelopes are merged through the mapping.

          Budget items get renamed and recreated, so the same envelope can appear under several names. Each original name is mapped to a canonical item and a category; the mapping file lives beside the ledger and is the thing to edit (Settings) if a category looks wrong.

          RefreshingEvery figure is rebuilt from the stored exports.

          Each month the two exports for the closed budget month are added to the set, the ledger and these files are rebuilt, and the page redraws from them. Nothing on the page is typed in by hand, so a corrected export changes every dependent figure at once.

          Reading the colorsGreen and red mark direction, not judgment.

          Green and red mark direction, not judgment: green is money kept or a category under its envelope, red is money drawn down or an envelope exceeded. Chart series take the brand palette in order (series 1 through 8 in the theme).

          NotesNotes from the exports are kept in the ledger, never shown here.

          The ledger carries every note from the exports, but notes are not shown on this page, because they can hold private details such as medication and people's names.
